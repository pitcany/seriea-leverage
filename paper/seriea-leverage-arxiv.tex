\begin{abstract}
Does a better forecast of final points establish a better forecast of survival?
We examine that distinction and, separately, the survival stakes of avoiding
defeat versus turning a draw into a win.
Using 7,220 Serie A fixtures from 2007/08--2025/26, we compare goals-based
Dixon--Coles forecasts, independent-Poisson shots-on-target ratings, and a
documented closing-price benchmark. Parameters and combinations are selected on
2013/14--2018/19; 2,660 subsequent matches form a historical evaluation period.
We evaluate final-points and survival forecasts for all clubs at three dates
across seven seasons, using the applicable head-to-head and playoff rules.
On March 1, structural-model points mean absolute error is \SeasonMarchMAE{},
versus \SeasonMarchBaselineMAE{} for a league-average Poisson comparator:
difference \SeasonMarchMAEDifference{} (95\% season-bootstrap interval
\SeasonMarchMAECI{}). Survival Brier scores are \SeasonMarchBrier{} and
\SeasonMarchBaseline{}, with difference \SeasonMarchDifference{}
(\SeasonMarchDifferenceCI{}). Points gains have intervals excluding zero at
all three dates; survival gains remain unresolved. An exploratory breakdown
uses the bottom eight clubs at each forecast origin.
For match forecasts, calibrated goals have ranked probability score
\GoalsRPS{}, versus \MarketRPS{} for the closing market.
For Fiorentina against Lecce, the simulated survival difference between a draw
and a loss is \LecceDrawBenefit{} percentage points, versus \LecceWinBenefit{}
between a win and a draw. Reweighting draws toward wins and losses yields a
break-even ratio of added win probability to added loss probability of
\LecceBreakEven{} (approximate 95\% simulation-only interval
\LecceBreakEvenCI{}). This illustrates how fixture stakes depend on the type
of probability change, without identifying a tactical or rotation effect.
The historical forecast comparison and the hypothetical probability trade-off
answer different questions; neither establishes a useful real-world policy.
The contribution connects outcome-specific forecast evaluation to an
application of established conditional-importance methods. Revised analyses
are retrospective, and prospective forecast and action validation remain
necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, application and prior work}
Clubs fighting relegation need probabilities of survival, while forecast
improvement is often summarized through match scores or final points. We ask
whether a transparent model that improves final-points predictions also
demonstrates better survival forecasts. Survival depends on the club's position
relative to other teams, their joint results and league rules; a better points
prediction alone does not establish that these probabilities improve.

We connect that forecast-evaluation question to a separate scenario question:
when does avoiding defeat have more survival value than turning a draw into a
win? A single win-versus-loss importance score does not distinguish these two
consequences. We retain both and derive the probability trade-offs needed for a
hypothetical change to improve survival. Historical forecasting performance and
the modeled consequences of assumed probability changes are different forms of
evidence. Neither identifies how an actual tactic changes match outcomes.

Our application is survival planning in Serie A, where a match's relevance
depends on rival results, the remaining schedule and league rules. We compare
the model with a league-average baseline for season forecasts and separately
with closing betting prices for individual matches.

The underlying methods are established. \citet{dc} introduced a low-score
dependence adjustment and time weighting for football scores.
\citet{egidi} combine historical information and bookmaker odds in a Bayesian
score model. \citet{reade} compare statistical and betting-market forecasts,
including forecast-encompassing analysis of scoreline probabilities.
\citet{wheatcroft} forecasts match statistics and finds information beyond odds
with a different rating and combination procedure. A negative result for our
shots model cannot overturn that evidence or generalise to expected goals.

Our match-importance definition is also established. \citet{scarf} develop
conditional tournament importance and discuss effort allocation.
\citet{link} describe the difference in the probability of a season consequence
conditional on winning versus losing and its earlier antecedents; they study
its association with player activity and pacing. \citet{delorenzo} explicitly
incorporate win and draw importance. We apply this established framework to
the asymmetry of Serie A survival stakes and do not claim a new importance
metric or an optimal tactical rule.

This paper contributes a retrospective comparison of points and survival
forecast performance, an exploratory breakdown by standings at the forecast
origin, and a documented match-level evaluation of goals, shots, pooling and
recalibration. The Fiorentina example separately distinguishes avoiding defeat from turning a
draw into a win, reports simulation uncertainty, and makes their hypothetical
probability trade-off explicit. Its contribution is the integration and
evaluation of these components, not a new forecasting family or causal
decision rule.

\section{Data, timing and evaluation protocol}
Football-Data provides results, shots on target and bookmaker prices
\citep{football-data}. The local snapshot contains 19 complete seasons,
2007/08--2025/26, each with 380 fixtures: 7,220 matches overall. Date, season and
home/away club identify each fixture. We check duplicate fixtures, self-matches,
score/result agreement and season completeness. One match lacks complete
shots-on-target data and is excluded from shots fitting.

Model forecasts use only matches dated strictly before their forecast origin.
Origins advance by 14 days; the training window covers the preceding 1,460 days.
The cap is a substantive modeling choice: at decay 0.002/day the oldest retained
observation still has weight about 0.054. A forecast can therefore precede
kickoff by up to 13 calendar days. Closing prices have later information,
including potentially lineups and injuries. This is an operational comparison
with the eventual close, not a comparison at identical information times.

The 2,280 fixtures from 2013/14--2018/19 select the goals-model decay,
temperature and forecast-combination parameters. The 2,660 fixtures from
2019/20--2025/26 evaluate the selected forecasts. Forecast origins still refit
club ratings as new past matches become available; combination parameters
remain fixed. Revisions were developed after inspecting the historical test
period. We retain the chronological split but do not describe the revised
analyses as an untouched or preregistered holdout.

\subsection{Closing-price provenance}
We use Pinnacle closing prices before 23 July 2025, then Bet365 closing prices;
average closing prices are an explicitly labelled fallback if needed.
Football-Data warns that Pinnacle prices became systematically stale from
23 July 2025. We ingest closing columns directly and never replace missing
closing prices with pre-closing snapshots. A source and timing label accompanies
every forecast. In the evaluated sample, all earlier seasons use Pinnacle
closing prices and all 380 fixtures in 2025/26 use Bet365 closing prices.

Shin de-vigging \citep{shin} converts complete three-outcome price books to
normalised probabilities. The market remains a forecast, not ground truth.
Changing bookmaker across seasons, the de-vigging assumption and unmatched
forecast times constrain interpretation. The six-season comparison excluding
2025/26 and a 2025/26 average-closing sensitivity are reported in the
reproduction outputs.

\section{Forecasts and statistical comparisons}
\subsection{Goals and shots models}
For home club $i$ and away club $j$,
\[
 \lambda_{ij}=\exp(\gamma+a_i-d_j),\qquad
 \mu_{ij}=\exp(a_j-d_i),\qquad \sum_i a_i=0.
\]
The goals model multiplies the independent Poisson mass by
\[
\tau(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
1-\lambda\mu\rho,&x=y=0,\\
1+\lambda\rho,&x=0,y=1,\\
1+\mu\rho,&x=1,y=0,\\
1-\rho,&x=y=1,\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
We maximise the weighted log likelihood with weights $\exp(-\xi\Delta t)$,
where $\Delta t$ is measured in days. The validation grid is
$\xi\in\{0,.002,.004,.0065,.009,.012,.018\}$ and selects \SelectedDecay{}.
The original value 0.0065 used half-week units in \citet{dc};
it corresponds to about 0.001857/day, not 0.0065/day. Our selected daily
rate has a half-life of approximately \HalfLife{} days.

Probability support must hold at every possible score, even unobserved low
scores. We therefore optimise a fraction $\eta\in[-.999999,.999999]$ of a
rate-dependent admissible interval. Let
\[
 B=\exp\{\max(\gamma,0)+\max_i a_i-\min_i d_i\},\quad
 C=\exp\{\gamma+2(\max_i a_i-\min_i d_i)\}.
\]
We set $\rho=\eta/B$ for negative $\eta$ and
$\rho=\eta\min(1,1/C)$ otherwise. These conservative bounds guarantee positive
low-score corrections for every pairing of the fitted ratings and the stated
fallback ratings. They may constrain the likelihood more than checking only
observed pairings. Prediction rejects invalid support rather than clipping
negative probabilities. Optimiser convergence and minimum forecast tau are
retained with the forecasts.

The shots model fits independent Poisson rates to home and away shots on target:
$\rho$ is fixed to zero during fitting, as well as in the converted goal grid.
Venue-specific league conversion factors are the weighted totals of goals
divided by weighted totals of shots on target in the same training window.
The shots model shares the selected goals decay for a controlled comparison;
we do not claim that this is the best possible shots specification.
Changing both target and dependence structure means this is not an experiment
that isolates target noise.

Both models use attack zero for an unseen club (the unweighted mean under the
identifiability constraint) and exposure-weighted mean defence. The shots model
uses the same rate-extraction path, preventing a different unseen-club fallback.
Score grids include 0--12 goals per side and are renormalised after truncation.
The season model uses raw score grids; the separately temperature-calibrated
three-way goals forecast is used only in match evaluation.

\subsection{Pooling is a restricted comparison}
For market probabilities $m$ and structural probabilities $s$, a logarithmic
pool is $p_k\propto m_k^{1-w}s_k^w$. We select $w\in[0,1]$ on validation log
loss, explicitly evaluating both endpoints. Multi-model weights use a
nonnegative simplex grid with spacing 0.05.

A zero weight establishes only that the structural forecast was not selected
within this family on validation data. A positive weight can compensate for
calibration error without adding match-specific information: even uniform
probabilities can soften an overconfident forecast. Conversely, a useful signal
can receive zero weight if its mapping into probabilities is misspecified.
A negative coefficient generally changes match-specific relative odds and is
not equivalent to a single temperature.

To distinguish a simple recalibration gain from a gain using structural
forecasts, we fit two nested multinomial-logit combinations. With away as the
reference class, for $k\in\{H,D\}$,
\[
 \log\frac{p_k}{p_A}
 =\alpha_k+\beta_M\log\frac{m_k}{m_A}
   +\beta_G\log\frac{g_k}{g_A}
   +\beta_S\log\frac{s_k}{s_A}.
\]
The market-only model fixes $\beta_G=\beta_S=0$. Both versions estimate the
same two class intercepts and market coefficient on validation data.
Bounds are $[-3,3]$ for intercepts, $[0,5]$ for $\beta_M$ and $[-2,2]$ for
structural coefficients; no test-set tuning is performed. Their held-out
score difference is the relevant comparison. This is still a restricted
combination model, not a general test of informational completeness.

\subsection{Scores, uncertainty and calibration}
The primary match score is the ranked probability score (RPS), using H--D--A
ordering and division by two; lower values are better. We also report log loss,
the sum-of-squares multiclass Brier score and accuracy. A uniform forecast's
argmax ties select H in the software: its displayed accuracy therefore equals
the home-win frequency, not the expected accuracy of random guessing.

Every comparison scores the same fixtures. We provide paired match-bootstrap
and paired season-bootstrap intervals using 10,000 resamples. Season resampling
retains dependence among matches in a season, but only seven clusters are
available. Neither procedure propagates model-estimation or specification
uncertainty. Intervals for the additional comparisons are descriptive, without
a multiplicity correction or an equivalence margin.

For each outcome we report equal-width-bin calibration, bin counts, descriptive
Wilson intervals, and calibration intercept/slope estimates with Fisher
intervals. The latter assume independent observations and are not a clustered
test of superiority. Macro one-versus-rest AUC describes discrimination;
entropy describes concentration. Neither is interchangeable with calibration.

\section{Season simulation and retrospective validation}
Each simulation independently draws a scoreline for every remaining league
fixture from the raw goals model fitted at the decision date. Ratings are
frozen throughout the remaining schedule. Final totals combine completed
results with these draws. This excludes future transfers, injuries, tactical
changes, parameter uncertainty and dependence induced by latent team-strength
updates.

Points-tied groups are ordered by their head-to-head points, head-to-head goal
difference, overall goal difference, goals scored and drawing lots. From
2022/23, ties at the title and survival boundaries trigger playoffs after the
mini-table selects the participants. The 2022/23 rule uses one neutral match.
From 2023/24, the title uses one match at the better-ranked club and survival
uses two legs, with the better-ranked club hosting the return
\citep{rules2022,rules2023,rules2025}. Equal aggregate goals go directly to
penalties: there is no extra time or away-goals rule. Playoff scoring uses
the same frozen ratings; penalty shootouts are modeled as 50/50. We assume the
scheduled home venue for title playoffs, excluding exceptional public-order
venue changes.

\subsection{Evaluation design}
We reconstruct forecasts for all 20 clubs on January 1, March 1 and May 1 in
each season from 2019/20 through 2025/26: 140 club-season observations per
horizon, 420 overall. Dates and comparisons were fixed for this revision after
the historical outcomes had been inspected. This is exploratory validation,
not a prospective trial.

For every origin we draw 10,000 seasons and evaluate survival Brier score,
final-points mean absolute error, empirical continuous ranked probability score
(CRPS) and central 90\% point-interval coverage. The comparator uses league-wide,
time-weighted home and away goal rates from the identical training window.
It preserves the current table and remaining fixtures but assigns no
club-specific strengths. It is a transparent baseline, not a claim to be the
strongest alternative.

The future fixture list is taken from the completed dataset, with realised
schedule dates; no future scores or closing prices enter the forecasts.
The season model has no time-varying future ratings, so future fixture dates do
not affect its rate estimates. Known points deductions are applied at the
origin. For Juventus in 2022/23 we use the 15-point sanction after January 20,
its removal after April 20, and the eventual 10-point sanction after May 22
\citep{juve15,juveappeal,juve10}. Future legal changes are not anticipated.
Final points include the actual sanction; actual survival labels include
Verona's win over Spezia in the June 2023 playoff. Errors can therefore
include unanticipated administrative changes.

\section{Fiorentina: avoiding defeat or chasing victory?}
The preceding analysis evaluates historical forecast quality. The following
illustration asks a separate question about the consequences of assumed
probability changes; it does not explain the historical forecast-score gaps.
At March 1, 2026, we fit the model using only prior results and simulate 20,000
season completions. Fiorentina's projected survival probability is
\FioSurvival{}, compared with \FioSimplified{} under the earlier
points/overall-goal-difference ranking applied to the same league draws.
A survival playoff is triggered in \PlayoffFrequency{} of simulations across
the league. That is a rule-trigger rate, not a prediction-error rate.
Expected final points are \FioPoints{}. The later observed finish is an
illustration, not evidence that one probabilistic forecast was calibrated.

For fixture $m$ and survival event $S$, define
\[
 q_{mr}=P(S\mid R_m=r),\qquad
 L_m=q_{mW}-q_{mL}.
\]
These quantities are extracted from one joint simulation, retaining
conditional sample counts and Monte Carlo standard errors.
The largest fixture importance is \TopOpponent{} (\TopImportance{}
percentage points). Inter's importance is \InterImportance{} percentage points.
To distinguish the two improvements involving a draw, define
\[
 U_m=q_{mW}-q_{mD},\qquad A_m=q_{mD}-q_{mL},\qquad L_m=U_m+A_m.
\]
Here $U_m$ is the survival gain from a win rather than a draw, and $A_m$ is the
gain from a draw rather than a loss. Both include the associated opponent
points and conditional scorelines; neither isolates the causal value of a
league point. Table~\ref{tab:stakes} shows the three fixtures with highest
total importance, Lazio as an illustrative contrasting profile, and Inter.
These examples were selected retrospectively; all 12 fixtures are reported
in the accompanying results.

Against Lecce, avoiding defeat has a survival difference of
\LecceDrawBenefit{} percentage points, versus \LecceWinBenefit{} for turning
a draw into a win. Against Lazio, the corresponding differences are
\LazioDrawBenefit{} and \LazioWinBenefit{}. This distinction describes the
consequences of results; it does not establish which changes a team can achieve.

Fixture importance is not the expected cost of resting a player. If a
hypothetical intervention changes only the W/D/L probabilities from $p_r$ to
$p'_r$ while preserving conditional scorelines, all other fixtures and the
playoff model, then
\[
 \Delta P(S)=\sum_{r\in\{W,D,L\}}(p'_r-p_r)q_{mr}.
\]
A shift of $\delta$ probability specifically from win to loss has cost
$\delta L_m$; shifts involving draws need the draw conditional as well.
In particular, consider moving probability $a\geq0$ from draws to wins and
$b\geq0$ from draws to losses, with $a+b\leq p_D$. Then
\[
 p'_W=p_W+a,\quad p'_D=p_D-a-b,\quad p'_L=p_L+b,
 \qquad \Delta P(S)=aU_m-bA_m.
\]
For $U_m>0$ and $b>0$, survival improves when $a/b>A_m/U_m$.
Lecce's estimated break-even ratio is \LecceBreakEven{}
(interval \LecceBreakEvenCI{}), compared with \LazioBreakEven{}
(\LazioBreakEvenCI{}) for Lazio. Thus the estimated probability trade-off
varies by fixture. These intervals do not establish a precise ordering of
all fixtures or account for uncertainty in a proposed action's effects.

The same hypothetical shift changes expected league points by $2a-b$.
For illustration, moving five percentage points of probability from draws to
wins and another five to losses adds \ScenarioPointsGain{} expected points.
It changes simulated survival by \LecceScenarioChange{} percentage points
against Lecce (interval \LecceScenarioCI{}), and \LazioScenarioChange{}
against Lazio (\LazioScenarioCI{}), whose interval includes zero.
The effects are small; they illustrate
that an expected-points improvement need not improve modeled survival.
The shifts are feasible in the empirical outcome distribution of each
displayed fixture, but are assumed scenarios rather than estimated tactics.
Each calculation changes one fixture separately; the effects cannot generally
be added across simultaneous changes. Appendix~\ref{app:stakes-uncertainty}
describes the simulation intervals, including the shared draw conditional.

Actual rotation can also alter score margins, later fatigue and other fixtures.
No player-level causal model is estimated here, so the table identifies
fixtures for further analysis rather than prescribes a lineup.

Under the independent-fixture, fixed-parameter simulator, conditioning on an
observed simulated result is equivalent to drawing the focal scoreline from
its conditional distribution and resimulating all other fixtures and
independent playoff randomness. It is not equivalent to fixing an arbitrary
scoreline, such as 1--0, and does not generally survive conditioning on shared
latent strengths.

\section{Limitations and implications}
The revised empirical claims are tied to the implemented models,
forecast timings and evaluation periods. Shots on target are an imperfect
chance-quality proxy; a shared decay and Poisson count model do not exhaust
their predictive potential. The closing benchmark is stronger in information
timing than a fortnightly structural forecast. A wider market/structural
combination class may perform differently.

The season analysis adds evidence beyond a single case, but remains
retrospective, small at the season level, and dependent on fixed-rate
assumptions. Improved survival forecasting against a league-average baseline
would not alone demonstrate practical benefit from player rotation.
Prospective validation should archive forecasts before outcomes, compare
stronger same-time baselines, and estimate intervention effects independently
of fixture importance. The accompanying study protocol specifies these steps.

Competition rules can change, and penalty strength and exceptional venue
decisions are additional modeling assumptions. League points, top-six position
and qualification for a particular European competition must not be equated;
the latter also depends on cup and eligibility rules outside this study.

Reproducibility materials include the implementation, regression tests,
forecast-level outputs, input hashes, exact dependency versions and scripts
generating the reported tables and figures. Raw source files are held locally;
public redistribution and the conference's data-sharing requirement need
permission review. No independent audit or large simulation verification is
claimed without a corresponding executable artifact.

\section{Conclusion}
The structural model improves final-points predictions against the
league-average comparator at all three dates, while survival-score gains
remain uncertain. Those results support evaluating forecasts against the
specific season outcome of interest, rather than treating points accuracy as
evidence of survival-probability quality. The origin-defined subgroup analysis
retains that retrospective, exploratory interpretation.

Separately, the Fiorentina example shows why avoiding defeat and turning a
draw into a win need different conditional values: a hypothetical probability
shift can improve expected points while reducing modeled survival. Its effects
are small and conditional on the simulator, and no tactic is shown to deliver
the assumed change. The match-level comparison likewise establishes neither
informational completeness of betting prices nor a decision-policy benefit.
Prospective forecast validation and independently estimated action effects
remain necessary.

\section{Simulation uncertainty for conditional stakes}
\label{app:stakes-uncertainty}
Let $v_r=\hat q_r(1-\hat q_r)/n_r$. Conditional on the W/D/L sample counts,
the independent season draws give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\hat U)=v_W+v_D,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\hat A)=v_D+v_L,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(\hat U,\hat A)=-v_D.
\]
Normal intervals use these variances. Ratio intervals use the Fieller set
\[
 (\hat A-t\hat U)^2\leq z_{.975}^2
 \left\{\operatorname{Var}(\hat A)-2t\operatorname{Cov}(\hat U,\hat A)
       +t^2\operatorname{Var}(\hat U)\right\}.
\]
If the denominator is poorly resolved, the set can be unbounded or disconnected;
the accompanying output retains these cases rather than forcing a finite
interval. The scenario variance is $a^2v_W+(a+b)^2v_D+b^2v_L$.
These are pointwise Monte Carlo approximations conditional on fitted
assumptions, not season-bootstrap intervals or simultaneous comparisons.
More simulations would not resolve uncertainty about the model or an actual
action. The full fixture report includes counts, covariance, interval status,
and feasibility checks using the empirical W/D/L frequencies.
